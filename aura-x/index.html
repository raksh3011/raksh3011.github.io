AURAX: a UAV that navigates without GPS

A software stack that flies a simulated long-range flying wing over real mountain terrain using only a LiDAR and an IMU — plans a verified terrain-following route, estimates its own position error, adapts the route and margins to that error, and returns to the launch point with no link to the ground.

AURAX ground station · 11 recorded simulations over eastern Ladakh and the Kashmir valley

This is the real browser ground station replaying logs produced by the C++ simulator. Planning and simulation run locally in the full version; the hosted demo replays 11 recorded flights (pick one from the REPLAY menu; a short guide opens on entry). Everything is a simulation — no hardware, no real sensor data; sensor models are documented stand-ins.

The problem

Small UAVs usually rely on a GNSS fix. Take it away (jamming, spoofing, denial) and position error accumulates while the aircraft must still avoid mountains, restricted airspace and unmapped obstacles, finish a task, and come home on a limited battery. A flying wing is efficient but has little control authority to spare, so poor routing wastes energy and margin.

The core idea: one uncertainty estimate, used everywhere

A single quantity — the predicted horizontal position uncertainty σ along the route — is mapped through the same small set of functions in every layer: global search cost, terrain-clearance margins, the independent route verifier, local obstacle avoidance and mission tasks. Change the navigation system and the route, margins, avoidance and tasks all change consistently.

What I'm showing here

  • System architecture — how planning, navigation, sensing and control layers interact, and what each is allowed to assume about the others (my role on the ICMOTA 2026 work).
  • Path planning & trajectory optimisation — terrain-following 3-D search, smoothing, altitude profile by dynamic programming, and a verifier that never lets an unchecked route fly.
  • Evaluation with honesty — benchmarks against ablations, estimator consistency (NEES), and documented known limitations.

Honest limits

  • Everything is simulated; LiDAR, odometry, scene-matching and detector models are stand-ins behind small interfaces, with assumptions documented in the repository.
  • The navigation uncertainty predictor is calibrated against the simulation and is optimistic in the tail; a thin wire missing from the map is a known collision case.
